2. The Bound-Setting Code

In dist_input_solve.gms (lines 33–37):

v_betas.LO("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,EYEARS)  $ p_supports(...)
    = 0.20 * p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,"Mean",EYEARS);

v_betas.UP("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,EYEARS)  $ p_supports(...)
    = 5.00 * p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,"Mean",EYEARS);

This assumes p_supports is positive:

  • If positive: LO = 0.20× (small), UP = 5.00× (large) → valid
  • If negative: LO = 0.20× (less negative, closer to 0), UP = 5.00× (more negative, farther from 0) → LO > UP, invalid!

Numerical Example from the Listing

v_betas(EAA_REL, OFARorg, REPMorg, 2010):

  • p_supports Mean ≈ −10.10
  • LO = 0.20 × (−10.10) = −2.02
  • UP = 5.00 × (−10.10) = −50.48
  • −2.02 > −50.48 → bound error

3. Why p_supports Becomes Negative — The Full Chain

The chain goes through three parameters, all computed in dist_input_prep_prior.gms:

Step A: p_SectorCostsFromEAA — Always Positive

p_SectorCostsFromEAA(est_r,YEARS) = DATA("%MSLONG%","EAAB",est_r,YEARS) ...

These come from EAA (Economic Accounts for Agriculture) and represent total national expenditures on each input category. They are inherently positive.

File: dist_input_prep_prior.gms, line ~34

Step B: p_totRev Goes Negative

For organic "remaining" inputs (REPM, REPB, INPO, etc.):

p_totRev(est_r_org,YEARS) = SUM(revenues from organic activities)
    - p_SectorCostsFromEAA("FEEDorg",YEARS)
    - p_SectorCostsFromEAA("SEEDorg",YEARS)
    - p_SectorCostsFromEAA("PLAPorg",YEARS)
    - p_SectorCostsFromEAA("IPHAorg",YEARS)
    - p_SectorCostsFromEAA("FERTorg",YEARS)
    - p_SectorCostsFromEAA("YANIorg",YEARS);

For Cyprus — a small member state with a tiny organic sector — the sum of expected revenues from organic activities is small, while the subtracted sectoral costs (feed, seed, fertilizer, etc.) can exceed the revenues. This makes p_totRev negative for the residual input categories in certain years.

File: dist_input_prep_prior.gms, lines 149–162

Step C: p_expCostsFromSectorRelations Becomes Negative

p_expCostsFromSectorRelations(E_PACT_org,est_r_org,YEARS)
    = p_SectorCostsFromEAA(est_r_org,YEARS) / p_totRev(est_r_org,YEARS) * ...

When p_SectorCostsFromEAA is positive and p_totRev is negative, the ratio is negative. This negative value flows through without any sign guard.

File: dist_input_prep_prior.gms, lines 429–443

Step D: p_supports Inherits the Negative Value

p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_i,"Mean",EYEARS) $ pact_to_i(E_PACT,EST_i)
    = p_expCostsFromSectorRelations(E_PACT,EST_i,EYEARS);

No sign check. The negative value is directly assigned.

File: dist_input_equations.gms, lines 324–325

4. Why Only Year 2010 and Only Certain Countries?

  • Year 2010 is the first year where Cyprus organic activity levels (and thus revenues) drop enough relative to sector costs to produce a negative p_totRev. Years 1998–2009 have either sufficient revenue or zero organic activity levels (skipping the solve altogether).
  • Small member states like CY with very small organic sectors are most susceptible, because a handful of organic activities generate minimal revenue while sector-level EAA costs are proportioned from the total (non-organic) sector.

5. The Bound-Widening Loop Makes It Worse

In dist_input_solve.gms (lines 134–140), there is an iterative widening:

v_betas.lo(...) = v_betas.lo(...) * 0.9**p_nSolved(years);
v_betas.up(...) = v_betas.up(...) * 1.1**p_nSolved(years);

When both bounds are negative:

  • Multiplying LO by 0.9 moves it toward zero (increases it)
  • Multiplying UP by 1.1 moves it away from zero (decreases it)

This further widens the LO > UP gap.

8. Possible Fixes

Option A — Guard the Bounds (symptom fix)

In dist_input_solve.gms (lines 33–37):

v_betas.LO("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,EYEARS) $ p_supports(...)
    = MIN(0.20 * p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,"Mean",EYEARS),
          5.00 * p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,"Mean",EYEARS));

v_betas.UP("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,EYEARS) $ p_supports(...)
    = MAX(0.20 * p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,"Mean",EYEARS),
          5.00 * p_supports("EAA_REL",E_PACT,EST_I,"Mean",EYEARS));

Option B — Clamp p_totRev Upstream (root cause fix)

In dist_input_prep_prior.gms (lines 149–162):

p_totRev(est_r_org,YEARS) = MAX(small_positive_epsilon, <existing calculation>);

Recommendation

A combination of both Option A and Option B provides the most robust approach. Option B prevents the economically nonsensical negative total revenue, while Option A ensures the solver never receives invalid bounds regardless of upstream data.
